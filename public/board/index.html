Design board · Workshop prototype v1

Ask many restaurants at once.
The first Yes gets your table.

A mobile prototype for Ulsan: your first choice is full and you can only walk. TrustMyChef lists trusted places nearby, asks the ones you pick by Instagram DM or AI call at the same time, and books the first one that says Yes. Sending requests comes with TrustMyChef Pass, ₩3,000 a month; the info and the map stay free. In the test we ask “Would you subscribe?” before trying and again after booking, and nothing is charged.

01

From a food map to instant booking

After the midterm: a map of expert picks brings people in, but searching is hard to charge for. People pay when we do something for them, such as getting a table right now.

AS-IS · low willingness to pay

Search

A map of places experts recommended in videos. Great for trust and for bringing users in, but there is no clear moment to pay.

TO-BE · high willingness to pay

Transaction

Booking and getting a seat now. Like CatchTable, the money is in booking management, not in finding the place.

Kakao Taxi style

Ask many, first Yes wins

One request goes to several restaurants at once. The first owner to tap Yes gets the booking. The others are cancelled for you.

03

Design principles

Five rules we checked every screen against.

Walk first

Minutes on foot come before anything else. Real routes, not straight lines.

Who said it

Every place shows who recommended it and why. Trust lies with people (midterm insight).

One tap, many asks

Pick places, choose how to ask, send once. No calling one by one.

Show the machine

Every simulated reply is labeled, and a system log shows what happens and why.

Easy words

Short sentences and common words, so international students can follow without help.

Validation in the workshop

Goal: find out which features people would pay for, and where the sales point is. Quantitative first: everyone tries the prototype and rates it on the phone (3–5 min). Qualitative next: post-it ideas on paper, grouped later in an affinity diagram.

Items: screen 1, screen 2, screen 3 and the whole service with the Pass. Y axis: “Would you pay for it?” (1–10). 6 or more counts as high.

Two kinds of data

  • Qualitative: post-it ideas (own ideas and the two “what if” questions) stay on paper. Groups place them on the paper 2×2; the team clusters them in an affinity diagram.
  • Quantitative: 1–10 ratings for the 4 items, plus “Would you subscribe? ₩3,000/month” (yes / maybe / no) before trying and after booking: the change shows the sales point.
  • Both are linked by the group (1, 2, 3, 4, Teaching).

What we collect

  • Only fields from our interview forms: gender, age (in full years), affiliation, meals out per month, apps used. All required; each has a “prefer not to say” or “other” option.
  • No name, phone, e-mail or account.
  • What they did in the prototype: places picked, DM vs AI call, subscribe answers, time to choose, result.
  • Results page (team key): one quadrant chart, means ± SD, share per quadrant, subscribe yes-rate before → after, filters, CSV export.

Copy guide: easy English

Many participants are international students. One idea per sentence, everyday words, numbers as digits, no idioms. Korean uses warm 해요체.

Rules

  • 12 words or fewer per sentence on screens.
  • Verbs people say: pick, ask, wait, book, walk, pay.
  • Every status has a word and a color, never color alone.
  • Say what is simulated: “Simulated reply”, “test, no charge”.
  • Korean: 해요체, no jokes; same meaning as English, not word-for-word.
